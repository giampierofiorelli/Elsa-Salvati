Career coaching & guidance

Finding your direction at work, in the life you're building here.

One-to-one career coaching in English with Elsa Salvati, a psychologist who has worked in HR for over ten years. For anyone who has moved to Italy, or is about to, and wants work that fits the rest of their life. Sessions are online, by video call.

01 · Situations

Where you might be starting from.

  1. 01

    Applications go nowhere, and everyone says it's who you know

    You've sent CV after CV and can't even get an interview. A CV written for another job market can come across very differently to a recruiter here, and contacts matter when you have almost none yet.

  2. 02

    Your qualifications and experience don't seem to count

    Years of work and a degree that opened doors at home now seem to carry little weight. Getting a qualification formally recognised is a separate process, and it takes time.

  3. 03

    You followed your partner, and your own career went on hold

    The move made sense for both of you. Now your partner has a job to go to and you're the one rebuilding from scratch, often in a language you're still learning. How you spend your days affects everything else, at home as well as at work.

  4. 04

    You're moving soon and want a plan before you arrive

    You'd rather know what's realistic before you land: which roles are open to you, how much Italian they need, and what to prepare while you're still abroad.

  5. 05

    You have a job, but it isn't going anywhere

    You've settled in, the work is fine, and there's no obvious next step. You may be wondering whether to push for more where you are, change field, or leave Italy altogether.

If this feels harder than you expected, you're not alone: 42% of expats in Italy rate the local job market negatively. InterNations Expat Insider 2026

02 · How it works

It starts with your situation, not with your CV.

Proposed method: to confirm with Elsa.

Career coaching with Elsa is a series of one-to-one video sessions. It's practical work on your career, led by a psychologist, and it isn't therapy. What you do in the sessions depends on where you're starting from.

  1. 01

    Your whole situation comes first

    Elsa starts by looking at your skills and experience and what you want from work. Then come the things you have to plan around, such as how much Italian you speak, family, a visa or where you'd like to live. Moving country changes your working life and your home life at the same time, so a career plan that leaves one of them out rarely holds up for long.

  2. 02

    Then a realistic look at what's open to you

    An ad that asks for "fluent Italian" doesn't always mean it. Elsa has spent years on the other side of job ads, so she can help you tell a hard requirement from a wish list, and say plainly how much Italian the roles you want are likely to need. From there you compare the options that are realistically open, and decide whether a course or a new qualification would actually change your chances.

  3. 03

    The work itself depends on where you are

    Still deciding on a direction: you won't start with your CV. You work out which directions make sense for you.

    Skills assessment tools, if any: to confirm.

    Wanting to progress in your current job: the focus is on how to move forward where you are, and what that would take.

    Actively looking for work: your CV, target companies, the job platforms worth your time, LinkedIn and interview practice.

  4. 04

    A plan you review at every session

    Each session ends with concrete next steps, and the next one begins with how they went, so the plan changes as you find out what works.

    At the end, a written plan with your options and next steps; a fictional sample to show here: to confirm.

03 · What it covers

What career coaching with Elsa can cover.

After the first sessions, you and Elsa agree which of these you need.

  • CV writing: a CV aimed at the roles you want, and at the people who will read it.
  • Job search: choosing target companies and the platforms where it's worth looking.
  • Interview preparation: mock interviews with feedback, so the real thing feels familiar.
  • Skills and experience assessment: what you bring, and how to describe it to an employer here.
  • Networking and LinkedIn: building contacts from almost nothing, and a profile that supports what you're aiming for.
  • Career direction: working out which way to go when more than one path is possible, or none looks clear.
  • The stress of a career transition: room to deal with the pressure of a job change or a new start, as part of the coaching.
  • Training and new skills: deciding whether a course, a qualification or more Italian is worth it for your goals.
  • Job offers: if you get an offer, you can go through it together, understand what it includes and prepare to negotiate.
  • Relocation pointers: first pointers on housing and paperwork for your move. Only if the service is confirmed.

04 · Limits

What coaching can't do, and when someone else can help.

  • No one can guarantee a job. Elsa helps you prepare and decide; she doesn't put clients forward to employers.
  • Rule on how this fits with the practice's recruitment and outplacement work for organisations: the practice decides.
  • Some questions need a specialist. Formal recognition of foreign qualifications, legal questions about a contract, and visa or move paperwork need the relevant office or professional. Job offer work with Elsa isn't legal advice.
  • The brief includes legal aspects and workers' rights: the client decides; any referral network to confirm.
  • While a qualification is being recognised, Elsa can help you decide what to do with your experience in the meantime.
  • When it's more than a career question. Months of rejections can wear down anyone's confidence, and sometimes what's left goes beyond career work. Then Elsa will say so and, if you'd like, suggest Monica Federico, the practice's psychotherapist, who has an MSc in work and organisational psychology. With your consent, Elsa and Monica can talk to each other.
  • Therapy in English: to confirm. How information is shared, and its limits: to define with the practice and check with your own adviser. If you're still abroad: the rules for online services, especially psychological ones, may depend on the country you're in; to check with your own adviser.
