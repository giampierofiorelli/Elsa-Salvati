Where you might be starting from

Applications go nowhere, and everyone says it's who you know. You've sent CV after CV and can't even get an interview. A CV written for another job market can come across very differently to a recruiter here, and contacts matter when you have almost none yet.

Your qualifications and experience don't seem to count. Years of work and a degree that opened doors at home now seem to carry little weight. Getting a qualification formally recognised is a separate process, and it takes time.

You followed your partner, and your own career went on hold. Now your partner has a job to go to and you're the one rebuilding from scratch, often in a language you're still learning.

You're moving soon and want a plan before you arrive. You'd rather know what's realistic before you land: which roles are open to you and how much Italian they need.

You have a job, but it isn't going anywhere. You may be wondering whether to push for more where you are, change field, or leave Italy altogether.

If this feels harder than you expected, you're not alone: in InterNations' Expat Insider 2026 survey, 42% of expats in Italy rated the local job market negatively.

It starts with your situation, not with your CV

Career coaching with Elsa is a series of one-to-one video sessions: practical work on your career, led by a psychologist. It isn't therapy.

Your whole situation comes first. Elsa starts by looking at your skills and experience and what you want from work. Then come the things you have to plan around, such as how much Italian you speak, family, a visa or where you'd like to live.

Then a realistic look at what's open to you. An ad that asks for "fluent Italian" doesn't always mean it. Elsa has spent years on the other side of job ads, so she can help you tell a hard requirement from a wish list, and say plainly how much Italian the roles you want are likely to need.

The work itself depends on where you are. Still deciding on a direction: you won't start with your CV. Wanting to progress in your current job: the focus is on how to move forward where you are. Actively looking for work: your CV, target companies, LinkedIn, building a network and interview practice. If you get an offer, you go through it together and prepare to negotiate.

A plan you review at every session. Each session ends with concrete next steps, and the next one begins with how they went.

What coaching can't do, and when someone else can help

No one can guarantee a job. Elsa helps you prepare and decide; she doesn't put clients forward to employers. Formal recognition of foreign qualifications, legal questions about a contract, and visa or move paperwork need the relevant office or professional.

Months of rejections can wear down anyone's confidence, and sometimes what's left goes beyond career work. Then Elsa will say so and, if you'd like, suggest Monica Federico, the practice's psychotherapist, who has an MSc in work and organisational psychology.
