01 · Situations

Some difficulties began with the move, others long before it.

  1. 01

    Something that began, or got worse, after the move

    Worry has taken up so much room that there isn't much left for anything else. Your heart races before an ordinary phone call, and you've started avoiding places and people to keep it from happening. Or getting up has become the hardest part of the day, and the things you used to enjoy now feel like effort.

  2. 02

    Something you've carried for years

    Certain thoughts come back however hard you push them away, and only a ritual quiets them for a while: checking, washing, counting. Or relationships, at work and at home, keep going wrong in ways that feel familiar, with emotions that swing fast and leave you exhausted.

  3. 03

    Something that happened to you still hasn't let go

    It comes back at night, or lives on in a body that never quite relaxes, whether it happened last year or long ago.

  4. 04

    Something that has started to run your days

    What you eat, or avoid eating, takes up much of your thinking, and meals have become something to manage or hide. Or alcohol, a substance or a habit you meant to cut down on keeps winning, and you've stopped telling people how much.

You don't need a name for what you're going through to get in touch. The free first meeting is for finding out whether psychotherapy with Monica could fit; the first sessions are where you work out together what's going on.

03 · How it works

Cognitive behavioural therapy, explained without jargon.

Proposed method, length and frequency: to confirm with Monica.

Cognitive behavioural therapy, often shortened to CBT, looks at how your thoughts, feelings, actions and body keep each other going. A worried thought makes your body tense; the tension makes you avoid something; avoiding it makes the worry stronger next time. In CBT you learn to spot these loops in your own life and try other ways of responding, in sessions and between them.

Monica also works with a bio-psycho-social approach. Alongside your thoughts and feelings, she takes into account your physical health and your circumstances, your work included. A difficulty that seems to be about one thing is often tied up with the others.

  1. 01

    The first sessions: understanding the difficulty

    Monica asks about what's happening now, when it started and what makes it better or worse, and what you'd like to be different. At the end of this phase she shares how she understands the difficulty, and you agree on goals together.

    Assessment questionnaires, if any: to confirm with Monica.

  2. 02

    Then the work itself

    Sessions focus on the goals you agreed. You might keep a short record of the situations that set off the difficulty, or practise facing something you've been avoiding, step by step. Sometimes you test whether a belief you hold about yourself stands up. Between sessions there is usually something to practise.

    Specific techniques: to confirm with Monica.

  3. 03

    Regular reviews, and a planned ending

    You and Monica check progress against your goals along the way. Towards the end, you prepare for how to keep what you've learned and what to do if things get harder again.

    Usual length and frequency: to confirm with Monica.

  4. 04

    Choosing the language

    Therapy asks you to describe thoughts and feelings precisely, so it's worth choosing a language you're comfortable in.

04 · When another professional is needed

When a doctor or another service is needed.

  • Medication and psychiatry. Blooming doesn't offer psychiatry. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor and can prescribe medication; a psychologist or psychotherapist who isn't a doctor can't.
  • For some difficulties, therapy and medication are used together: to confirm with Monica. When and how Monica suggests seeing a psychiatrist or your GP: to confirm with Monica.
  • Physical risk. Some eating disorders and addictions also affect your physical health and need medical care alongside therapy, or a specialist service.
  • How Monica handles these situations, and when she refers: to confirm with Monica.
  • Emergencies. Blooming isn't an emergency service. If you or someone else is in danger, call 112 or 118, or go to the nearest emergency department (Pronto Soccorso).
  • When the difficulty is mainly about work. Monica can suggest Elsa for career or workplace questions, alongside therapy or after it. With your consent, they can talk to each other.
  • To confirm with the practice. How information is shared, and its limits: to define with the practice; check with your own adviser.
