Workplace coaching

Coaching for the job you already have, in a country that's still new to you.

You have a job, and something about it isn't working: the meetings in Italian, perhaps, or a manager whose expectations you can't read. Workplace coaching with Elsa Salvati, a psychologist who has worked in HR for over ten years, is one-to-one work on your current job, online, in English. A hard week at work tends to follow you home, so the sessions also look at how it's affecting the rest of your life.

01 · Situations

Work can get harder in ways nobody warned you about.

  1. 01

    You work in Italian all day, and it costs you more than anyone sees

    You follow most of the meeting, but by the time you've built the sentence, the discussion has moved on. Colleagues may think you have less to say than you do. Your Italian is improving; what you need is a way to take part that doesn't leave you drained by Wednesday.

  2. 02

    Your manager or your team works in a way you weren't prepared for

    Decisions seem to get made somewhere you aren't, and feedback arrives in a form you don't know how to read. The gap is real, and it can turn into friction with one person that slowly takes up all your attention.

  3. 03

    A reorganisation has changed your job around you

    A merger, a new manager or a role rewritten without much discussion. You're still doing your work, but you're no longer sure what's expected of you or where you stand.

  4. 04

    You're leading a team for the first time

    Perhaps you were promoted from within, or hired to manage people who have worked together for years, possibly in a language that isn't your first. Delegating feels new, and so does giving feedback to people who used to be your peers.

  5. 05

    You're stuck, and the motivation has gone

    You do what's needed and not much more, and you've stopped expecting anything to change. Before deciding anything big, you'd like to understand what's happened and what could shift.

02 · How it works

Coaching with Elsa starts from your situation, then one or two goals you choose.

Proposed method: to confirm with Elsa.

Workplace coaching is focused, practical work on your current job and your next steps there, in one-to-one video sessions. It's led by a registered psychologist, and it isn't therapy: the attention stays on what you want to change at work and how.

  1. 01

    The first sessions: your situation, then your goals

    Elsa asks about your role and what a good week at work would look like. She also asks about what surrounds the job, such as how much Italian you use and how settled you feel, because that shapes what's realistic. Together you choose one or two goals you'll recognise when you reach them, for example speaking up in the weekly meeting, or handing over work you've been holding on to.

    Assessment tools or questionnaires, if any: to confirm with Elsa.

  2. 02

    Then work chosen for your situation

    What you do in the sessions depends on the goal. You might rehearse a difficult conversation before you have it, or prepare how to make your point in a meeting held in Italian. You might map where your time and energy go in a typical week, to see what's eating into the hours you need for your main work. If you're new to leading a team, you can plan your first one-to-ones or work out how to give feedback people can act on. When the difficulty is a manager or a team that works differently from what you know, you look at what they seem to expect and how you'd like to respond.

  3. 03

    Between sessions, you try something at work

    Each session ends with one or two things to try. The next one starts with how they went, so the work stays tied to your real job.

  4. 04

    A regular check on progress

    Every few sessions you and Elsa look back at the goals: what has moved, what hasn't, and whether they still make sense. Coaching ends when you've reached your goals.

    Or when you decide to stop: to confirm with Elsa. Written summary at the end: to confirm with Elsa.

03 · What it covers

What workplace coaching with Elsa can cover.

After the first sessions, you and Elsa agree which of these to work on.

  • Stress at work: what's driving it, and what you can change in how you work.
  • Conflict: a disagreement with a colleague or manager that keeps coming back, and how to approach it.
  • Productivity: where your time goes, and how to protect the work that matters.
  • Relationships at work: with colleagues, your manager and the people you manage.
  • Managing change: finding your footing after a reorganisation, a new role or a new manager.
  • Getting unstuck: when you keep circling the same problem without moving.
  • Motivation and engagement: understanding why the interest has gone, and what might bring it back.
  • Leadership: leading people for the first time, or leading a team that's changing.
  • Career development where you are: the skills and relationships that would help you move up in your current organisation. If the question is which direction to take, that's career guidance.
